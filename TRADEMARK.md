## Why the name is protected

The model itself is open: map to it, build on it, extend it. The **name** is
different. "ACDM" is how an advancement shop knows the model it is mapping
against is the real standard and not a private fork wearing the same label.
Protecting the name protects that trust.

## What you may do without asking (fair / nominative use)

You do not need permission to truthfully refer to the project:

- State compatibility or lineage: "built on ACDM", "imports ACDM",
  "maps to ACDM".
- Make truthful conformance claims once the conformance program is published.
- Redistribute the materials unmodified, keeping the name and the notices the
  license requires.
- Refer to the project in articles, talks, documentation, and comparisons.

Keep it factual, and do not style the reference to imply you are the project
or that the project endorses you.

## What needs permission

- Using the Marks in the name of your product, company, service, or domain in
  a way that implies it **is** the standard or is endorsed by it (for
  example, "ACDM Pro", "the ACDM CRM", `acdm-cloud.com`).
- Presenting a **modified** version of the model under the ACDM name. If you
  change the model, you may say "based on ACDM" or "an ACDM derivative", but
  you may not call the result ACDM itself. Forks rename.
- Using the logos in altered form, or any use likely to confuse adopters
  about the source of the standard.
- Any "ACDM-certified" or "ACDM-conformant" badge or mark, which is reserved
  for the project's conformance program.
